The wedding welcome sign

One of my most diverse builds: a wedding welcome sign that brought CNC design, laser cutting, staining, tiny glue-ups and mounting together.

The idea

A layered wooden welcome sign for a wedding, using a National Park Service-inspired motif. The aspiration was a personal event object whose form and lettering belong together, with the build also serving as a way to explore tools and techniques. The interesting visual quality is physical layering: the design has depth, edges and shadows, rather than existing only as a flat graphic.

How it works

This is a physical build, not software to launch locally. Its use is the object’s actual purpose described above.

Inside the build

Layered construction

The layered wooden form is confirmed. It opens a useful discussion about separating a graphic into physical parts, but the number of layers, material thicknesses and attachment method still need actual build records.

Lettering in a material

Personalized signage has to work at viewing distance and as a close-up object. The story can show placement and edge quality once photographed without inventing the cutting method.

A referenced visual language

The National Park Service motif is a design reference, not evidence of affiliation or an original invention of that visual language.

What I learned

Think in depth

The learning topic is translating a graphic idea into a layered object whose shadows and material edges contribute to the result.

Design for a setting

A welcome sign is used by arriving guests. Its role in the event helps explain decisions about hierarchy and legibility without assuming dimensions or fabrication details.
