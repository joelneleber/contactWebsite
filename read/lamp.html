The idea

A personalized wedding gift combining photographic panels, a wooden top and base, light and engraving. This is one of my favorite builds. The aspiration was to make a physical object with personal meaning while learning across materials and techniques. Unlike the programming entries, its strongest evidence will be the actual object: how the panels read when lit, how the wood frames them and where the personalization belongs.

How it works

This is a physical build, not software to launch locally. Its use is the object’s actual purpose described above.

Present actual whole-object and detail photographs when available; no generated illustration is a substitute for an evidence photo.

Inside the build

The photograph becomes part of an object

The confirmed build uses photographic lithophane panels. The story can explain how images, transmitted light and a surrounding structure work together without assuming a particular printer, panel material or panel-making process.

Wood as structure and framing

The wooden top and base give the illuminated panels a physical home. Actual detail photographs are needed before describing joinery, tolerances or the order of assembly.

Personalization

The engraving is confirmed. Its exact machine, tooling, settings and finishing sequence are not yet documented, so this story does not assume it was CNC-cut or laser-engraved.

What I learned

Design across materials

A useful learning thread is coordinating image, light and wood as one object rather than treating them as unrelated parts.

Make the purpose visible

A wedding gift has a specific recipient and context. The visual story can show the personalization without publishing private photographic details that the people involved would rather keep private.
